# make-slides rule ledger

Every distinct rule or idea in the eleven source files plus the named evidence, sorted against our material in `~/oss/agents-library` (all 18 references, explain-simply, design-ui, write-tech-doc).

Totals: 295 rows. COVERED 64, GAP-SHARED 25, GAP-SLIDES 133 (about 40 of them say "merged with" another row, so make-slides needs roughly 90 distinct rules), REFUSE 73.

Key to the columns:

- **sources**: anth = anthropics pptx, fs = frontend-slides, mk = marketingskills deck-frameworks, lenny = lenny-exec.md + lenny-art.md, mm = minorun slide-story, hum = humanizer, pf = pamelafox (outline / review), dex = dexhorthy html-presentations, ev = the named outside evidence. "added" marks a row I added beyond the seed list.
- **evidence**: measured (numbers published), described (a write-up or method, no numbers), claimed (an assertion with no method). This follows `references/research-sources.md` "Authority score".
- **verdict**: COVERED, GAP-SHARED (goes in a reference), GAP-SLIDES (goes in make-slides), REFUSE.
- **where**: for COVERED, the file and the line text; for GAP-SHARED, the file and section; for GAP-SLIDES, the proposed make-slides section; for REFUSE, the reason.

Proposed make-slides sections used below: **Deck type**, **Discovery**, **Story**, **One slide**, **Titles**, **Evidence and text**, **Speaker notes**, **Build (Google Slides API)**, **Reuse an existing deck**, **Timing**, **Check**, **Slide tells**. make-slides invokes explain-simply and points at the references, so nothing COVERED is restated in it.

## Contradictions and the side we take

1. **"Every slide needs a visual element" (anth A26) against exec decks, mm and Mayer.** Lenny's dinosaur brain and the Amazon memo say executives need few facts and a clear recommendation, often as text. mm says a text-led slide keeps any figure at illustration size, and Mayer's coherence studies (measured) show decorative pictures lower understanding. Our own `visuals.md` line 21 already says "Plain text is enough when the idea is already obvious." We take Alley's assertion-evidence rule (measured): every content slide has a sentence title and evidence, and the evidence can be a chart, diagram, table, screenshot, worked example or real quote. Decoration never counts as evidence. Divider and ask slides may be text only.

2. **Answer first or answer last.** mm (M25 to M28) bans agendas, upfront conclusions and overview maps, and Lenny's triptych says to tell them three times. Lenny's Minto, SCQA, buy-in and "movie arc" rows say to lead with the answer, and our `explain-simply` rationalisation table allows a summary at the top only "if every word in it is already defined". We decide by deck type. A decision, update or exec deck, and any deck that will be read without the speaker, puts the answer and the ask on the first content slide, because the meeting can be cut short and the reader may stop early. A live teaching talk may run in discovery order, but every slide title still states that slide's own claim. Neither type gets a recap slide that only restates (`machine-tells.md` "Closer that repeats").

3. **"Don't default to blue" and palette advice (anth A21 to A25, A41, A48).** These are taste, and they conflict with `design-ui/SKILL.md` "Start from what exists". We build in Google Slides with the company's template, so the template sets colour and type. We keep only the floors: contrast, one accent, colour never carrying meaning alone.

4. **Fonts: fs bans Arial, Inter and system fonts; anth recommends Arial and Calibri as safe.** Each is right for its own renderer and taste. We use the template's fonts, which Google Slides renders itself, so neither rule applies.

5. **Vary layouts (anth A37) against one visual system (anth A38, fs F27) and mm's warning about pouring content into templates (M8).** Variety for its own sake is not a goal. One system for the deck, with each slide's layout chosen by its role.

6. **Icon rows in coloured circles (anth A27, A31) against mm's "icon plus bold heading plus one line, three across, conveys nothing" (M8).** We side with mm, backed by Mayer coherence (E2).

7. **Annotate screenshots (mk K10, and our explain-simply line 54 "Annotate every element") against "no text explaining what an image is" (mm M49).** Both hold once we separate two kinds of text. A cue that points at the part that matters stays (Mayer signaling, measured). A caption that names what is already visible goes (Mayer coherence). A reading deck can carry more annotation than a speaking deck, because no speaker is there to narrate.

8. **Logos, analyst recognition and awards as proof (mk K11) against borrowed authority (`machine-tells.md` line 27, hum §17).** We take our rule: a named result with its number, or nothing.

9. **Hype on slides (mm M24 "100x", M54 "easy!", M75) against `machine-tells.md` stock pitch and inflated weight.** We take ours. The audience is engineers and leaders who discount hype.

10. **Varied heading registers with "!" (mm M50, M51) against consistent action titles (McKinsey, Alley).** Titles state claims; they should vary in length and shape (M52), without hype punctuation. The Japanese register rules do not carry over to English.

11. **Re-render only the changed slides and stop (anth A50) against a full confirming round (`preview.md` step 6).** We take `preview.md`: a template or layout edit moves slides nobody touched.

12. **Click-to-reveal (dex D6, fs F30 motion) against the PDF export losing it (fs F35).** We use separate build-up slides (mm M30), which also survive async reading and the Slides API's lack of animation support.

13. **"Thank You / Questions?" as the last slide (dex D8) against ending on the ask (mk K17, mm M22, M23).** The last slide carries the one ask.

14. **One-line headings (mm M47) against two-line action titles (McKinsey).** One line for speaking decks, at most two for reading decks.

15. **Noun labels on figure slides (mm M51) against sentence headlines everywhere (Alley, measured).** We take Alley, keeping figure-slide titles short.

16. **Lenny's own bad-news template puts results fifth (L20), while its checklist says open with "this project failed" (L17).** We take the checklist: the miss goes on slide one.

17. **mm's "what is X?" naming first and abstract, concrete, then analogy (M16, M17) against our "show one instance, then name it" and "mechanism first, analogy as a bridge" (`visuals.md` lines 5, 19, 57).** We keep ours for engineering audiences and borrow mm's one-line "roughly speaking" takeaway as the step's last line.

18. **mm opens the body with a working example (M19) while explain-simply opens with "why it exists".** These are compatible for talks: a short "here is what you can build" hook, then the problem. For decision decks the problem comes first.

19. **mm "the later date is not always newer" (M72) against a "follow the newest source by date" rule.** That rule holds for successive versions of one document. When two copies grew in parallel, compare item by item.

20. **mm "no source note on the slide" (M58) against `writing-voice.md` "Facts with links. Numbers with their source."** Speaking decks put sources in the speaker notes; reading decks put a short source line on the slide.

21. **anth body text at 14 to 16 pt (A35) against large type for speaking decks (fs F6, mm M40).** The size floor depends on the deck type.

22. **Lenny "clean, opinionated recommendation" (L12, L18) against `respect-human-attention.md` "Your recommendation is not the user's".** The deck carries the user's recommendation, in the user's words; the agent drafts it only when asked and marks it as a draft.

23. **Humanizer lets a writing sample override the dash rule (H6) against the user's standing no-dash rule.** The user's rule wins.
