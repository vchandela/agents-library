# Career guardrails

The user's career rules, kept out of project repos and out of a thread's plan. Read this when the user discusses a deliverable, a priority, a summary, a thread's scope, a promotion, credit, ownership, burnout, or how to spend the week.

**How to use it.** In those conversations, name the guardrail in play in one line and ask its question. Never lecture. The one that runs at every priority call is glue work: separate the deliverable from the glue, ask its test, and put the same test in every work thread's brief and every reviewer's opener, so a pair does not default-own glue.

| Trigger | The pattern | The question to ask the user | The alternative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Promotion | A retention or empowerment decision, not a reward for effort. The company-critical project, the staff slot, the manager campaigning. "Just a matter of time" for a year is a signal. | "Is this a retention or empowerment decision, or a reward for effort?" | Ask for the project, the slot or the manager's campaign; otherwise move. |
| Glue work | Invisible glue: incidents, coordination, unowned fixes. Default-owning it hides the gap. | "If I stopped, would it get fixed, or would I be blamed?" | Fix the incident, make the ownership gap visible, and do not absorb the role. |
| Care and heat | A public thread carries the facts and the recovery. The root cause belongs in a private conversation. | "Is this public or private?" | Keep the heat private; if in doubt, do not show anger. |
| A mistake | The first message is the facts, not the apology. The apology and "never again" go in the follow-up. | "What happened, and what is the recovery?" | Facts first; the apology after. |
| High-impact work | By invitation, and the trust ratchet cannot be skipped. A flagship without trust may come with a shadow-lead who takes the credit and leaves the blame. | "Am I trusted enough for this, and is the ownership clear?" | Build the trust first, or clarify the ownership. |
| The week | About 20% unallocated, because impact is outlier-driven. Every learning bet needs a deliverable and a date. | "What is the deliverable and the date? What is unallocated?" | Protect the slack; bet with a date. |
| Usefulness | Usefulness is addictive and gets exploited. Aim it at the management chain, not the ticket queue. Burnout can come from work that does not feed the wiring. | "Is this useful to the people who decide, or just the next ticket?" | Point the effort up the chain. |
| Meaning | At a large company meaning is scale, not mission. Rituals are not meaning; the rest comes from outside work. | "Am I asking this place for meaning it cannot give?" | Look outside work. |
| Long hours | The 100-hour claim is grindslop unless the extra hours buy a shipped output. | "What would ship from the extra hours?" | No ship, no hours. |
| Credit | Credit accumulates when you give it away. Shared projects get talked up; solo projects reflect well on nobody. | "Who can share this?" | Share the credit. |
| Agents | Ask the agent one question while it works; the answer often reveals a mistake. | "What did you just do, and why?" | Ask every 30 seconds while it runs. |
| Agreement | Put the agreement last: "but yes", not "yes but". | "Which order did I put it in?" | Agreement last. |

**In the board and the briefs.** At every deliverable, priority, summary or board discussion, say which item is a deliverable and which is glue, and ask the glue test. Keep glue out of the week's commitments unless the user chooses it. Every new thread's brief and every reviewer's opener carries the same test.

Source: the user's career notes, passed 23 November 2026. The full notes stay in his own vault; this is the distilled set the coordinator applies in conversation and in thread briefs.
