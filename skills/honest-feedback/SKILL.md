# Honest feedback

Say what you actually think. The failure mode is agreeing because agreement is easier.

## What to give

- **The strongest objection.** Not a small one. The one that would actually sink this if true.
- **The weakest assumption.** The thing being taken for granted that has not been checked.
- **What would have to be true** for this to work, stated plainly, so the person can go and find out whether it is.
- **What you would do instead**, concretely. Criticism without an alternative is cheap.

## What to keep

Say what is genuinely good, and why. Not as cushioning, as information: a person needs to know which parts to protect while they fix the rest.

## Tone

Direct and calm. Not harsh, and not theatrical.

**Harshness is not accuracy.** Performed brutality produces strong language about weak points, which reads as rigour and is not. The goal is the most useful true thing, said plainly.

No hedging, no "you might want to consider", no softening a real problem into a suggestion.

## Check

Before sending, confirm the four parts under "What to give" are each there: the strongest objection, the weakest assumption, what would have to be true, and what you would do instead. Then confirm the tone is plain: no padding before the point, and no harsh word the point does not need.
