# Review a product page

A page has one job: a stranger understands it, believes it, and does the one thing. Judge it in that order, because a fix lower down cannot rescue a failure higher up.

## 0. Write the positioning card first

Copy cannot be judged against nothing. Read the product's positioning doc if one exists; otherwise draft it from the site and the repo, mark each guess, and ask only about the guesses.

- **Who**, in one line, and who it is not for.
- **The status quo**: what they do today without you. It is the real competitor, and it is usually a spreadsheet, a friend's advice, an agency or doing nothing.
- **The foil**: the old way you replace, named.
- **The one value vector** where you are plainly better, in a way the reader cares about. Being "better" in general is a trap; be different on one axis.
- **Proof** you can show, and **the top three objections**.
- **The four forces**: what pushes them off the status quo, what pulls them to you, the habit that holds them, the anxiety about switching. Copy that only adds pull and ignores anxiety stalls.
- **Their words**: phrases real users use, from reviews, support, forums. Write in those, not yours.

## 1. Review in order of impact

1. **Five seconds.** From the first screen alone: what is it, who is it for, why now. If a stranger cannot say all three, stop here; everything else is decoration.
2. **The headline.** Prefix it with "Now you can". If the result is not both compelling and true, rewrite it. It names a new ability, specific enough to be checked, and matches what brought the reader here (the ad, the search, the link).
3. **One primary action per screen.** The button names what they get ("See my score"), not what they do ("Submit"). Secondary actions look secondary.
4. **Proof beside each claim.** Specific, attributed, with numbers and their source. Never invented: a fabricated testimonial or statistic is a legal problem as well as a trust one.
5. **Objections answered where they arise**: price beside the price, "will this work for me" beside the promise, "what if it doesn't" beside the button.
6. **Friction.** Every field, step and choice must earn its place. Ask for commitment after showing value, not before.
7. **The first run.** Time to the first real win (the aha) is the metric. Let them do it rather than watch a tour. Empty states teach the next action. Show progress already started. End each session on a win: people remember the peak and the end.

## 2. Check the words

Apply `references/writing-voice.md` and `references/machine-tells.md`. Clarity beats cleverness; benefits beat features; one idea per section.

**The perception gap.** Read each key line as each audience. "Move fast" is momentum to one reader and risk to another; the user and the person paying hear different things. Frame for the one who decides.

## 3. Report

Findings in impact order. Each one: what is wrong, the evidence (quote the line, name the screen), and the fix as rewritten copy, not advice. Split into quick wins and bets.

Be Bayesian about what converts. A finding from the list above is a strong prior, not a measurement. Say how confident you are, and for each bet name the cheapest test that would settle it (a five-second test with three strangers, an A/B test, a funnel step to watch).

## Check

Show only the first screen to someone who has never seen the product. Can they say what it is, who it is for, and what to do next, in their own words? If not, the page has not been fixed yet.
